## Takeaways

### Typical widths by style (from the rows above)

- Plain comfort-fit, women's: 1.5 to 4 mm on offer (Brilliant Earth sells 1.5, 2, 3 and 4 mm; Blue Nile/James Allen platinum at 4 mm). Plain, men's: 4 to 7 mm in the Brilliant Earth line, 6 to 8 mm for alternative metals.
- Knife-edge: 2 to 2.5 mm in the listings seen (James Allen sells 2, 2.5 mm).
- Milgrain: 3 mm plain (Valerie Madison); 3.3 mm when stone-set (Camellia).
- Eternity and pave: 2.0 to 2.6 mm for slim shared-prong or pave, 3 to 3.7 mm for bezel, channel and larger shared-prong, 3.6 to 4.7 mm for French-pave with 3 to 5 ct. total weight.
- Contour or chevron: 1.4 to 2 mm (Chevron Ring 1.4, Yvette 1.5, Corinne 1.7, Olivetta 2). Nested stack: 3 mm.
- Vine/twist: 2.5 mm. Braided: 3 mm.
- Men's: 6 to 8 mm is the common range; Manly Bands' own guide says most men's bands sit at 6 to 8 mm and offers 4 to 10 mm.
- Thickness (height) was almost never stated. The only figure found is the 1.6 mm height of the OroSpot braided band (3 mm wide). Treat band thickness as a gap; do not invent it.

### How contoured bands sit against heads

- Brilliant Earth reviews of the Lunette Nested stack say it sits flush against round, oval and pear engagement rings, with one reviewer noting the curve suits larger centre stones best (search result, https://www.brilliantearth.com/Lunette-Nested-Diamond-Ring-Stack-White-Gold-BE613/).
- Ritani describes curved bands (1.8 mm, 2 mm) as made to follow its halo rings (search result, https://ritani.com/collections/womens-curved-wedding-rings; page itself returned 403). James Allen's halo page advises pairing halos with a curved band that follows the halo contour (search result, https://www.jamesallen.com/engagement-rings/halo-rings).
- In real sets the band is often a plain straight or slightly curved pave band rather than a deep contour: Esme and Fancy Halo use straight 1.5 and 2 mm comfort-fit bands, Chamise uses a 1.7 mm contoured band with a basket head, Petite Twisted Vine Halo uses a vine band described as sitting flush. A chevron or V band (1.4 to 1.5 mm) is the other common answer for a low solitaire.
- Not found in this batch: any dimensional data on the gap clearance for a given halo height or stone size. That would have to come from a fit test in the app.

### Metal hardness and durability (limited evidence)

- Only retailer statements were captured here. Blue Nile says its 4 mm platinum comfort-fit band cannot be resized; Brilliant Earth and James Allen say eternity bands generally cannot be resized because stones circle the band (Blue Nile product page, search summary of Brilliant Earth eternity listings).
- The Robinson's page for the tantalum/titanium Royal band and the Brilliant Earth Merle Black page describe titanium or tantalum outer shells with a gold or beveled interior; no hardness numbers were on those pages.
- Price spread by metal at 6 mm men's: black zirconium $380 list, tungsten 8 mm $260 list, tantalum/titanium 8 mm $705, palladium 6 mm $3,500, titanium+18K gold 6 mm $2,090. Palladium's price at Market Street is far above typical sale figures from other sellers ($1,400 to $3,200 on Allurez per search results), so treat it as a high outlier.
- Hardness and brittleness comparisons (tungsten carbide vs tantalum vs zirconium vs gold) are general knowledge and were NOT verified from a cited source in this batch.

### Data cautions

- Several Brilliant Earth set pages were read via the en-gb site; the structured data was reported in USD and matched the US price for Sienna ($4,280), but check currency before using.
- Two Overstock/Manly Bands rows (Savant, Fitzgerald) come from search snippets because the page returned 403.
- Set center stones are mostly "setting only"; carat and cut are null where the retailer left the stone to the buyer.
- Ritani and several Blue Nile set pages were unavailable (403, sold out or no price), so no Ritani or Blue Nile bridal set is included.
