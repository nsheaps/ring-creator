# Glossary

Plain-language definitions of the words you will meet in jewellers' shops and in Ring Creator. Where a term has a page in the app or another guide, there is a link.

## A

**Accent stone.** Any small stone that supports the centre stone: halo stones, side stones or pave on the shank. Set on the Accents tab. See [Building a ring](#/docs/building-a-ring).

**Art Deco.** A design movement of roughly the 1920s and 30s. In rings: geometric shapes, step cuts, platinum, milgrain and openwork.

**Aspect ratio.** A stone's length divided by its width. A round stone is 1.00, an oval of 1.40 is longer than it is wide. See [Stone cuts](#/docs/stone-cuts).

**Asscher.** A square step cut with large clipped corners and a deep pavilion.

## B

**Band.** In this app, the wedding band worn beside the engagement ring. The part of the engagement ring that circles the finger is the shank. See [Wedding bands and fit](#/docs/wedding-bands-and-fit).

**Basket.** A head with a woven or lattice gallery of metal under the stone. See [Settings and mounts](#/docs/settings-and-mounts).

**Bezel.** A thin metal rim wrapped around the stone's girdle. The most protective way to hold a stone.

**Bow-tie.** A dark, bow-shaped shadow across the middle of some oval, pear and marquise stones, caused by the way light leaves the stone.

**Brilliant.** A faceting style of many small triangular and kite-shaped facets, designed to return as much light as possible.

**Bypass.** A shank or head where the two ends pass each other instead of meeting, curling around the stone. See [Shanks and styles](#/docs/shanks-and-styles).

## C

**Cabochon.** A smooth, domed, unfaceted polish, often used for opal.

**Carat (ct).** A unit of weight for gems: 0.2 grams. Not a measure of size. See [Choosing a stone](#/docs/choosing-a-stone).

**Cathedral.** Arches that rise from the shank on either side of the stone to cradle it, like a cathedral's vaults. Used both for shanks and heads.

**Channel setting.** Stones set side by side in a groove between two metal walls.

**Chevron.** A V-shaped band that dips to make room for a low solitaire.

**Clarity.** How free a stone is of internal marks (inclusions) and surface marks (blemishes), graded from FL to I.

**Claw.** Another word for a prong, especially a prong with a pointed, claw-like tip.

**Cluster.** Several small stones grouped to look like one larger stone.

**Colour grade.** The D to Z scale for colourless diamonds. D-F is colourless. In the app, grades are grouped D-F, G-J, K-M, N-R and S-Z.

**Comfort fit.** A band domed on the inside so it slides over the knuckle more easily.

**Contour band.** A band curved to follow the shape of the engagement ring's head. See the Fit tab.

**Crossover.** A shank whose two strands cross below the stone.

**Crown.** The top part of a faceted stone, above the girdle.

**Culet.** The tiny facet or point at the bottom of a stone. Old cuts often have a large, flat culet.

**Cushion.** A square or rectangular cut with rounded corners, like a pillow.

**Cut.** Two meanings. In the app, "cut" is the stone's shape (round, oval and so on). In gemmology, it is also the quality of proportions and polish. See [Stone cuts](#/docs/stone-cuts).

## D

**Dainty.** A slim, delicate shank or band.

**Depth.** A stone's height from table to culet, usually given as millimetres or as a percentage of its width.

**Dispersion (fire).** The splitting of white light into rainbow colours inside a stone. Moissanite has more than diamond.

**Double halo.** Two concentric rings of accent stones around the centre stone.

## E

**East-west.** A stone laid across the finger, with its long axis parallel to the band. Opposite of north-south. See the Placement controls in [Building a ring](#/docs/building-a-ring).

**Emerald cut.** A rectangle with clipped corners and long stepped facets.

**Eternity band.** A band set with stones all the way around (full eternity) or part of the way (half or three-quarter eternity).

**Eye-clean.** A stone with no inclusions visible to the unaided eye, even if it has some under magnification.

## F

**Facet.** One of the small flat polished surfaces on a gemstone.

**Filigree.** Fine, lace-like openwork of twisted or pierced metal.

**Finish.** The surface texture of the metal: polished, satin, matte, hammered, sandblasted, brushed, engraved or milgrain. See [Metals and mixed metals](#/docs/metals-and-mixed-metals).

**Flush setting.** A stone sunk into the metal so its table is level with the surface. Also called a gypsy setting.

## G

**Gallery.** Two meanings. In jewellery, the open space under the stone between the head and the shank. In the app, the [Gallery](#/gallery) page of example rings.

**Girdle.** The narrow band around the widest edge of a stone, where the crown meets the pavilion. Prongs and bezels grip the girdle.

**Green gold.** A pale greenish-yellow gold alloy.

**Gypsy setting.** See flush setting.

## H

**Half bezel.** A bezel that wraps only part of the stone's edge.

**Halo.** A ring of small stones around the centre stone.

**Hallmark.** A stamp that shows a metal's purity and often the maker.

**Hammered.** A finish of tiny dimples, made with a hammer or imitating one.

**Head.** The part of an engagement ring that holds the centre stone. Also called the setting or mount.

**Hidden halo.** A halo placed below the centre stone so it is visible from the side but not from above.

## I

**Inclusion.** An internal feature in a gemstone, such as a crystal, a feather or a cloud.

**Inner diameter.** The measurement across the inside of the ring, in millimetres. In the app, inner diameter = 11.63 + 0.8128 x US size. See [Wedding bands and fit](#/docs/wedding-bands-and-fit).

## K

**Karat (k).** The measure of gold purity out of 24 parts. 14k is 58.3% gold.

**Kite.** A four-sided cut with two long and two short sides, like a kite.

**Knife edge.** A band that rises to a ridge on the outside.

## L

**Lab-grown.** Made in a laboratory, with the same chemistry and structure as the natural stone, in the case of lab-grown diamond and sapphire. See [Choosing a stone](#/docs/choosing-a-stone).

## M

**Marquise.** A long, boat-shaped stone with a point at each end.

**Matte.** A flat, non-reflective finish.

**Melee.** Very small diamonds or other stones, used for halos, pave and accents.

**Milgrain.** Tiny beads of metal along an edge. Also the name of a finish and a band style.

**Mixed metals.** Using different metals in one ring or set, such as a platinum head on a rose gold shank.

**Mohs scale.** A 1 to 10 scale of scratch resistance. Diamond is 10. See [Choosing a stone](#/docs/choosing-a-stone).

**Moissanite.** A lab-made silicon carbide gem, hard and very fiery.

**Mount.** Another word for the head or setting.

## N

**Nickel.** A common metal allergen found in some white gold alloys.

**North-south.** A stone with its long axis pointing along the finger toward the fingertip. The traditional orientation.

**Notched.** A band with a cut-out where the engagement head sits.

## O

**Offset.** In the app, a slider that slides the stone along the band by up to 4 mm either way.

**Old European.** An early-twentieth-century round brilliant with a high crown, chunky facets and a visible culet.

**Old mine.** An earlier, cushion-shaped antique brilliant with a large culet.

**Orientation.** Whether a stone is placed north-south or east-west.

## P

**Palladium.** A white platinum-group metal that is lighter than platinum.

**Pave (pavé).** Small stones set very close together so little metal shows.

**Pavilion.** The bottom part of a faceted stone, below the girdle.

**Pear.** A teardrop-shaped stone with one point.

**Platinum.** A dense, naturally white, hypoallergenic metal. 95% pure in jewellery.

**Prong.** A small metal claw that holds the stone. Also called a claw.

## R

**Radiant.** A rectangle or square cut with clipped corners and brilliant faceting.

**Raise.** In the app, the setting height: the gap between the shank top and the base of the stone, 0 to 5 mm.

**Rhodium plating.** A thin bright-white coating applied to white gold. It wears and can be renewed.

**Ring size.** The size of the ring's inside. In the app, US sizes 3 to 13 in half steps.

**Rose cut.** An antique faceted dome with a flat base and no pavilion.

**Rose gold.** Gold alloyed with copper for a pink colour.

**Rotate.** In the app, a slider that turns the stone about its vertical axis, by up to 45 degrees either way.

## S

**Satin.** A soft, low-sheen finish.

**Seat.** The spot on the shank where the head sits, or the notch in a head where the stone rests.

**Setting.** The way a stone is held, or the head itself.

**Shank.** The part of the ring that goes around the finger.

**Shared prong.** A prong that holds two neighbouring stones in a band.

**Shoulders.** The parts of the shank beside the head.

**Side stone.** A stone set beside the centre stone, such as in a three-stone ring.

**Solder.** The metal alloy used to join parts. Where two different metals are soldered, the join is a place to inspect over time.

**Solitaire.** A ring with a single stone and no accents.

**Split prong.** A prong divided into two tines at its tip.

**Split shank.** A shank that divides into two strands near the stone.

**Stacking.** Wearing several rings together on one finger.

## T

**Table.** The large flat facet on top of a stone.

**Tension setting.** A setting that holds the stone by pressure from the band instead of prongs.

**Three-stone.** A ring with a centre stone and a smaller stone on each side.

**Tilt.** In the app, a slider that tips the stone toward the fingertip or knuckle by up to 25 degrees.

**Toi et moi.** French for "you and me". Two stones side by side.

**Trellis.** A head with crisscrossing prongs.

**Trillion.** A triangular cut.

**Twisted.** A shank or band made of strands wound around one another.

**Two-tone.** A ring made of two metal colours.

## V

**V-prong.** A V-shaped prong that wraps a pointed corner, as on a pear or marquise.

**Vintage.** Old or old-style. In the app, vintage designs use milgrain, filigree and antique cuts.

## W

**White gold.** Gold alloyed with white metals and usually plated with rhodium.

**Width.** The measurement across a band or shank along the finger axis. Set in millimetres.

## Z

**Zircon.** A natural gem with high sparkle (not to be confused with cubic zirconia, which is a manufactured stone). In the app, a 7 on the Mohs scale.
