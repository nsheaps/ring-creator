# Stone cuts

The cut (shape) is the first thing people notice. Pick it on the **Stone** tab. Each shape has its own sparkle style, its own vulnerable spots and its own idea of what proportions look best.

Two terms before we start:

- **Aspect ratio** is length divided by width. A round stone is 1.00. An oval at 1.40 is 40% longer than it is wide. The aspect slider on the Stone tab appears for shapes that can vary, and its limits come from each cut.
- **Facet style** describes how the surface is cut. _Brilliant_ cuts have many small kite and triangle facets built to flash. _Step_ cuts have long parallel facets like a staircase, giving mirror-like flashes and a clear view into the stone. _Rose_ cuts are a flat base and a faceted dome with no pavilion. _Cabochon_ is smooth and polished with no facets.

The aspect ranges below are typical trade figures, not a promise of what every stone looks like.

## Quick comparison

| Cut             | Facet style       | Typical aspect | Vulnerable corners  | Looks bigger than its weight? |
| --------------- | ----------------- | -------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Round brilliant | Brilliant         | 1.00           | None                | No, but the sparkle benchmark |
| Princess        | Brilliant         | 1.00 to 1.05   | 4 sharp             | Medium                        |
| Cushion         | Brilliant (mixed) | 1.00 to 1.20   | 4, rounded          | Medium                        |
| Oval            | Brilliant         | 1.30 to 1.50   | None                | Yes                           |
| Emerald cut     | Step              | 1.30 to 1.50   | 4, clipped          | Yes, large table              |
| Asscher         | Step              | 1.00           | 4, clipped          | Medium                        |

## Round brilliant

![Round brilliant]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/round-brilliant.png)

::component[cut:round-brilliant]

The classic. Its symmetry returns light from every angle, so it is the most sparkly shape per carat and the one most often given a formal quality grade. It has no corners to chip. It costs more per carat than fancy shapes because more rough crystal is lost cutting it. **Best for** sparkle-first buyers and almost any head. It is also the most forgiving shape for imperfect colour and clarity.

## Princess

![Princess]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/princess.png)

A square brilliant, modern and crisp. Its four sharp corners are the weak spot: prongs (or a V-prong or bezel) should cover each corner. **Best for** people who want a square face with lots of sparkle. Turn it 45 degrees with the Rotate control for a diamond-on-point look.

## Cushion

![Cushion]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/cushion.png)

A pillow-shaped square or rectangle with softened corners. It reads warm and vintage, with chunky flashes. Rounded corners chip less than a princess. **Best for** vintage and halo looks. Squarer cushions look bold. More rectangular ones look elegant on the hand.

## Oval

![Oval]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/oval.png)

An elongated brilliant with no corners. It makes fingers look longer and has a large face for its weight. Watch for the "bow-tie": a dark band across the middle of some ovals, caused by how light leaves the stone. Check a real stone. **Best for** everyday wear, solitaires and halos.

## Emerald cut

![Emerald cut]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/emerald-cut.png)

::component[cut:emerald-cut]

A rectangle with clipped corners and long stepped facets. It shows clarity and colour more openly than a brilliant, so aim for a better clarity grade, and you get the elegant hall-of-mirrors effect. The clipped corners are sturdier than sharp ones but still deserve protection. **Best for** Art Deco and minimalist looks. East-west placement is striking.

## Asscher

![Asscher]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/asscher.png)

A square step cut with large clipped corners, similar in feel to an emerald cut but more geometric and deeper. Strong Art Deco association. **Best for** vintage-inspired designs in platinum or white metals. Protect the four corners.

## Radiant

![Radiant]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/radiant.png)

A rectangle or square with clipped corners and brilliant facets: the sparkle of a brilliant in the outline of an emerald cut. Sturdy and lively. **Best for** buyers who like the emerald outline but want more fire.

## Pear

![Pear]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/pear.png)

A teardrop with one point. The point is fragile, so a prong, a V-prong or a bezel tip is non-negotiable. **Best for** people who like an elongated, graceful look. Point it toward the fingertip (north-south) for the classic hand-lengthening effect, or turn it east-west for a modern one.

## Marquise

![Marquise]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/marquise.png)

A long, pointed oval, like a boat. It covers more surface per carat than almost any other cut, but both points are exposed and need protection. **Best for** statement rings and cluster or bypass designs.

## Heart

![Heart]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/heart.png)

Romantic and instantly recognisable. It needs a decent size (larger than about half a carat) for the shape to read clearly. The cleft and the bottom point are both delicate. **Best for** sentimental choices. Use a three-prong or bezel that protects the point.

## Trillion

![Trillion]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/trillion.png)

A triangle, often used as side stones flanking a centre in a three-stone ring. All three corners are vulnerable. Bezel or V-prongs preserve the geometry. **Best for** bold geometric rings and side stones.

## Baguette

![Baguette]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/baguette.png)

A narrow rectangle with step facets. Rarely a centre stone, but beautiful as side stones in channel or tapered three-stone settings and in Art Deco eternity bands.

## Trapezoid and tapered baguette

![Trapezoid]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/trapezoid.png)
![Tapered baguette]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/tapered-baguette.png)

Step cuts that narrow towards one end. As side stones in a three-stone ring the narrow ends point at the centre stone, so the pair hugs a round or oval centre. A trapezoid is short and broad; a tapered baguette is long and slim. Choose either as the accent cut on the Accents tab with a three-stone head. The corners are exposed, so protect them with prongs or bezels.

## Vintage cuts: old European, old mine, rose

![Old European]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/old-european.png)
![Old mine]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/old-mine.png)
![Rose cut]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/rose-cut.png)

**Old European** is a round with a small flat bottom (culet), a high crown and chunky facets, cut by hand in the early twentieth century. **Old mine** is an earlier, cushion-shaped relative with an even larger culet. Both give a candlelit, glittery pattern rather than a laser-white one. **Rose cuts** go back centuries: a flat bottom and a faceted dome, lower and softer, with a hazy glow. All three suit vintage and Art Deco designs, and pair well with milgrain, filigree, rose gold and platinum.

## Modern unconventional cuts: hexagon, kite, shield

![Hexagon]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/hexagon.png)
![Kite]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/kite.png)
![Shield]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/shield.png)

These are mostly found at small studios and in coloured or salt-and-pepper stones. They are striking and individual. Corners are exposed, so bezels are the usual choice. Hexagon has six corners; the kite's points are especially exposed at the ends; the shield has a flat top and a pointed base.

## Cabochon

![Cabochon]({{base}}screenshots/components/cut/cabochon.png)

A smooth, domed, unfaceted polish. It is the usual cut for opal and other opaque or translucent gems, and for star stones. With no corners it is safe, and a bezel protects the edge. **Best for** soft or opaque materials.

## Orientation: north-south or east-west

North-south means the stone's long axis runs along the finger, toward the fingertip. East-west means it lies across the finger, parallel to the band. It only matters for non-round shapes. In the builder, set it under **Head > Placement > Orientation**.

![Placement controls]({{base}}screenshots/pages/builder.png)

| Orientation | Effect                                                                                             | Good with                                                 |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| North-south | Makes fingers look longer and slimmer. The traditional placement.                                  | Oval, pear, marquise, emerald cut                         |
| East-west   | Looks modern and wide, and sits lower on the hand. A long stone can feel bigger across the finger. | Emerald cut, oval, marquise, radiant, cushion (elongated) |

Some practical points on east-west placement: a very long stone on a small finger can overhang the sides. Check it against your ring size in the Ring view with the Top camera preset. Wide stones also snag more easily on clothing than a compact north-south one.

The **Rotate** slider (minus 45 to 45 degrees) turns the stone about its own vertical axis, which is how you set a princess on a diamond angle.

## How to choose

- **I want maximum sparkle with minimum worry:** round brilliant or oval.
- **I want the most size for the money:** oval, pear, marquise or emerald cut, since elongated shapes show more surface per carat.
- **I want vintage character:** cushion, old European, old mine or rose cut.
- **I want a geometric, architectural feel:** emerald cut or Asscher.
- **I am hard on my hands:** avoid pointed shapes (pear, marquise, heart) or choose a bezel.
