# Wedding bands and fit

A wedding band lives next to your engagement ring for decades, so it should be comfortable, durable and in harmony with it. Turn the band on with **Include matching band** on the **Band** tab, then adjust the style, width and thickness. The **Fit** tab sets your ring size and how the band sits against the head.

**Eternity types.** Stones go around either part or all of the ring:

| Type                   | Stones                      | Comfort and practicality                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Half eternity          | Across the top half         | Comfortable, easy to resize, the usual stacker                                                                |
| Three-quarter eternity | About three quarters around | A compromise: more sparkle, some room to resize                                                               |
| Full eternity          | All the way around          | Maximum sparkle. Stones press against neighbouring fingers and the palm, and sizing is generally not possible |

**How the stones are held:**

- **Channel:** stones sit in a groove between two walls. Smooth, sturdy and snag-free.
- **Pave:** tiny stones set close together. Sparkly but with more small parts.
- **Shared prong:** each prong holds two stones. Airy and bright.
- **Bezel band:** each stone wrapped in a tiny rim. Very secure and modern.

**Bigger and mixed stones.** Eternity and shared-prong bands can grade their stones from the largest at the top down to smaller ones at the ends (Stone sizes: Graduated), and can fix the number of stones for a five- or seven-stone band. Raise the setting (Raised setting above 0) to let the stones be wider than the band itself. Curved (contour) and chevron bands can carry stones that follow the bend, largest at the point. **Alternate with** turns every other stone into another gem, such as sapphire between diamonds. The **Bamboo** band has raised nodes like a cane of bamboo and can hold a small stone in each.

Retailer data shows slim shared-prong or pave bands at about 2.0 to 2.6 mm, bezel, channel and larger shared-prong around 3 to 3.7 mm. Stone and accent choices (material, cut, size) are on the **Accents** tab.

## Contour, flush and notched: how a band sits against the head

On the Fit tab you can choose how a band meets the engagement ring.

| Fit            | What it does                                                       |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Auto (default) | Bands that can curve will curve around the head. Others sit flush. |
| Flush          | The band stays straight and touches the shank. Best for low heads. |
| Contour        | Forces a curve where the style allows it.                          |

![Fit tab]({{base}}screenshots/pages/builder.png)

Why it matters: a tall head, a halo or a big cathedral will not let a straight band sit tight. Either the band curves around it (contour), dips (chevron), is notched, or you accept a visible gap. Retailer notes say curved bands are made to follow halo rings, and several real sets use a straight band with a basket head. A chevron or V band is the other common answer for a low solitaire. We did not find reliable data on gap clearances, so use the 3D Set view and the Top camera to judge for yourself, then ask your jeweller to test a real fit.

The band cards show a "contour" tag on styles that can curve around a head. If you want a curve and your style cannot bend, choose one that can: contour curved, chevron or notched.

## Matching metals and widths

**Metal.** Wedding bands usually match the engagement ring shank. Two different metals rub together over time, and the softer one wears faster, so mixed pairs need more care ([Metals and mixed metals](#/docs/metals-and-mixed-metals)).

**Width.** Common pairings:

| Engagement shank    | Typical band             |
| ------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Dainty, 1.4 to 2 mm | 1.5 to 2 mm band         |
| Medium, 2 to 2.5 mm | 2 to 3 mm band           |
| Wide, 3 mm and up   | A band within 1 mm of it |

Retailer sets show matching bands at about 1.5 to 2 mm. The width slider runs from 1.2 to 8 mm and thickness from 1.0 to 3.2 mm. Thicker means heavier, tougher and more comfortable on the underside. The most common reasons to match width: the two rings look like one piece, and they wear evenly. A wider band beside a delicate ring can look heavy.

**Karat and wear.** Wear a band of similar hardness to the engagement ring if you want them to age together.

## Ring size

Set your size on the **Fit** tab. The app supports US sizes 3 to 13 in half steps in the dropdown, each with its inner diameter in mm. The relationship is:

`inner diameter (mm) = 11.63 + 0.8128 x US size`

The default size is 6.5, which is 16.9 mm inside.

| US size | Inner diameter | Inner circumference |
| ------- | -------------- | ------------------- |
| 4       | 14.9 mm        | 46.7 mm             |
| 5       | 15.7 mm        | 49.3 mm             |
| 6       | 16.5 mm        | 51.9 mm             |
| 6.5     | 16.9 mm        | 53.1 mm             |
| 7       | 17.3 mm        | 54.4 mm             |
| 8       | 18.1 mm        | 57.0 mm             |
| 9       | 19.0 mm        | 59.5 mm             |
| 10      | 19.8 mm        | 62.1 mm             |
| 11      | 20.6 mm        | 64.6 mm             |

The engagement ring and band always share one size in the builder.

**How to measure.** Wrap a strip of paper around the base of the finger, mark where it meets, and measure the length. That is your circumference; divide by 3.1416 for the diameter and compare with the table. Measure at the end of the day, when fingers are warmest, and check both the knuckle and the base. Wide bands (over about 4 mm) feel tighter, so many people go up a quarter to half size. Your jeweller will confirm.

**Resizing.** Plain bands resize easily. Eternity bands generally cannot be resized because stones circle the band, and retailers say the same of some platinum comfort-fit bands. Tension rings are hard to resize, and some alternative metals cannot be resized at all.

## Stacking

Stacking means wearing several rings together.

- **Stacker trio** gives you a ready-made set of three slim bands.
- Keep widths similar so the stack looks like a design, or pick a deliberate contrast.
- Mixing metals in a stack is fine and fashionable. See the mixed-metal rules.
- Bypass and wide shanks need more thought than a plain shank.
- Put the softer, more delicate ring between sturdier ones to protect it.

## Eternity versus wedding band: a short guide

For daily life, a half eternity with channel or shared-prong stones is the most practical stone-set band. Full eternity is for people who love maximum sparkle and accept limited resizing. A plain comfort band is the most durable and the easiest to keep forever.
