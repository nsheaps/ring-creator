# Metals and mixed metals

Metal is about colour, strength, skin comfort and cost. Choose metals on the **Metals** tab: a separate metal for the head, the shank, the band and (for two-tone bands) the band's accent metal. Under "Match all to" a row of colour swatches sets every part at once.

![Metals tab]({{base}}screenshots/pages/builder.png)

## Reading the numbers

- **Karat (k)** says how much of an alloy is pure gold, out of 24 parts. 10k is 41.7% gold, 14k is 58.3%, 18k is 75%.
- **Hardness (Mohs)** is the app's scratch-resistance number for the metal. Higher hardness resists scratching, though softer, purer metals can be springier or dent differently. Gold is a soft metal and is always alloyed for rings.
- **Hypoallergenic** means unlikely to cause skin reactions. The usual culprits are nickel and, for some people, copper. Higher-karat gold and platinum-group metals are the common safe choices.
- **Price tier** is relative. The app stores a rough per-gram figure for each metal, "indicative finished-metal value" and not a quote. Weight matters as much as the figure, because platinum is much denser (21.4 g/cm3) than 14k gold (about 13) and so the same ring weighs more.

## Gold

![14k yellow gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/yellow-gold-14k.png)
![18k yellow gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/yellow-gold-18k.png)
![10k yellow gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/yellow-gold-10k.png)

**14k** is the everyday champion in the US: a balance of colour, strength and cost. **18k** is richer in colour and less likely to irritate skin, but softer, so it picks up scratches and its prongs may need earlier attention. **10k** is the hardest and cheapest, but paler and more likely to cause reactions.

### Rose gold

![14k rose gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/rose-gold-14k.png)
![18k rose gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/rose-gold-18k.png)
![10k rose gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/rose-gold-10k.png)

Rose gold gets its pink from copper. More copper means a deeper colour: 10k is the most coppery, 18k the most delicate pink. The colour is solid through the metal and never plates off. It flatters morganite, sapphire in warm colours and champagne diamonds, and is a vintage favourite.

### White gold and rhodium

![14k white gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/white-gold-14k.png)
![18k white gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/white-gold-18k.png)
![10k white gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/white-gold-10k.png)

White gold is yellow gold alloyed with white metals. On its own it is a slightly warm grey, so jewellers plate it with **rhodium**, a bright white platinum-group metal. New rhodium looks crisp and icy. With wear, especially on contact points, it thins and the warmer alloy shows through. A jeweller can re-plate in a short visit. The app's component notes suggest every one to three years. Platinum does not need plating.

### Green gold

![18k green gold]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/green-gold-18k.png)

18k green gold is a soft pale lime-yellow made by adding silver. It is rare and distinctive, a good choice for nature-inspired designs.

## Platinum group

![Platinum]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/platinum.png)
![Palladium]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/palladium.png)

**Platinum** is naturally white, dense and hypoallergenic. It holds prongs securely and does not wear away when scratched; metal is displaced rather than lost, so it gains a soft grey patina that can be re-polished. It is heavier than gold, so a platinum ring costs more even when per-gram prices are similar. **Palladium** is lighter than platinum and naturally white, in the same family, and a good middle path.

## Silver and alternative metals

![Sterling silver]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/sterling-silver.png)
![Titanium]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/titanium.png)
![Tungsten]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/tungsten.png)
![Black zirconium]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/black-zirconium.png)
![Tantalum]({{base}}screenshots/components/metal/tantalum.png)

**Sterling silver** is affordable but soft and tarnishes. It suits fashion rings and some antique silver-topped designs, but is not a practical lifetime choice. **Titanium** is very light and strong. **Tungsten carbide** is extremely scratch-resistant but tends not to be resizable, so check with the maker. **Black zirconium** gives a dark charcoal surface and **tantalum** a soft dark grey. These four are popular for men's bands, usually at 6 to 8 mm wide. Ask about the binder in tungsten if you have a metal allergy.

## Mixing metals

You can use a different metal for each part. The builder shows a "Mixed metals" chip when you do, with a reminder to ask your jeweller about solder lines and wear.

### Rules that usually work

1. **Pick a dominant metal and an accent.** Make about two thirds of the ring one metal. The accent should look deliberate.
2. **Warm head on a cool shank.** A yellow-gold head on a platinum shank warms a stone's sparkle while the band stays crisp. Retail listings include this pairing and also a platinum head on a rose-gold shank.
3. **Cool head, warm shank.** A white-metal head keeps a diamond bright while a rose or yellow shank adds warmth to the hand.
4. **Keep the head metal white if you want the whitest diamond.** The metal around the stone reflects into it.
5. **Match the karat.** If you mix yellow and rose, stay in the same karat so their softness and wear match.
6. **Tie it together.** Repeat one metal in a small detail, such as a two-tone band or milgrain edge.
7. **Think about wear.** Where two metals meet, a solder join is a place to inspect. Platinum prongs on a gold ring are strong but wear differently.

### Matching the band

Wedding bands usually match the engagement ring's shank. A different metal can work, but the metals will rub against each other over time, especially if one is much softer. See [Wedding bands and fit](#/docs/wedding-bands-and-fit). The band styles `mixed-metal-band` and `mens-inlay` in the builder show a second-metal selector.

### Three worked pairings

| Look             | Head            | Shank          | Band                             |
| ---------------- | --------------- | -------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Classic two-tone | Platinum        | 14k rose gold  | 14k rose gold                    |
| Warm vintage     | 18k yellow gold | Platinum       | Platinum with milgrain           |
| Soft modern      | 14k white gold  | 14k white gold | Mixed-metal band, white and rose |

## Finishes

A **finish** is the surface texture applied over any metal. Choose one for the engagement ring and one for the band on the Metals tab.

| Finish      | Look                        | Care                                    |
| ----------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Polished    | Mirror-bright. The default. | Shows fine scratches, easy to re-polish |
| Satin       | Soft low sheen              | Hides small scratches                   |
| Matte       | Flat, non-reflective        | Hides wear, can be refreshed            |
| Hammered    | Small dimples               | Hides scratches well                    |
| Sandblasted | Frosted, fine grain         | Wears smoother over time                |
| Brushed     | Fine parallel lines         | Easy to refresh                         |
| Engraved    | Cut lettering or pattern    | Can fill with dirt                      |
| Milgrain    | Tiny beads along edges      | Beads soften with wear                  |

The 3D preview shows engraving and milgrain as surface texture only. It does not carve text. Finishes change roughness in the preview, but real finishes depend on the jeweller's skill. A contrast of finishes, such as a polished head on a satin shank, is a quiet way to mix.

Some heads also offer an **oxidised (blackened) recess**, separate from the finish: the Art Deco plaque's **Recess finish** blackens the plate under its openwork, as on antique platinum plaques, so the piercing reads dark. It is the same metal; the spec and bill of materials list it as its own part with an "Oxidised recess" finish.

## Choosing quickly

- **Easiest all-round:** 14k gold or platinum.
- **Sensitive skin:** platinum, palladium, 14k or 18k yellow gold, titanium.
- **Lowest cost:** 10k gold, silver (for fashion), or alternative metals for bands.
- **Bright white forever:** platinum.
- **Warm, rich colour:** 18k yellow.
